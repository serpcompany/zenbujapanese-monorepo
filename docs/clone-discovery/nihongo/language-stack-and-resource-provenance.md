# Nihongo language stack and resource provenance

Status: research resolution for “Identify the Nihongo language stack and resource provenance”  
Verified: 2026-08-02  
Reference: the installed Nihongo iPhone app is the runtime authority; public distribution metadata was version 1.34.4 when checked

## Answer

Public, first-party evidence identifies only four parts of Nihongo's Search-rooted stack with high confidence:

1. **JMdict supplies dictionary data.** Nihongo's current App Store description credits JMdict, its universal links address word entries by JMdict sequence number, and its own release notes describe periodic JMdict data updates.
2. **Tatoeba supplies example sentences.** Nihongo's current App Store description credits Tatoeba, and its release notes describe JMdict and Tatoeba updates together.
3. **The CJK Dictionary Institute (CJKI) supplies pitch-accent data.** Nihongo introduced this as a separately purchased licensed dataset in version 1.20. Public evidence does not identify CJKI's exact delivered product, version, or Nihongo's matching/transformation layer.
4. **Google Cloud Vision and OCR.space are the disclosed online OCR providers.** Nihongo sends the selected photo to the configured provider. Its current FAQ also says Photo Lookup has a lower-accuracy offline mode, but no public source identifies that offline recognizer.

Everything else listed in the ticket is either behavior without an identified implementation, a historical in-app attribution that must be recaptured from the installed build, or unknown. In particular, public evidence does **not** identify Nihongo's search index, ranking weights, tokenizer, lemma resolver, deinflector, conjugation rules, handwriting recognizer, OCR word-grouping logic, kanji/radical base datasets, stroke-order dataset, TTS API, exact human-audio inventory, or current etymology compilation pipeline.

This means Zenbu can use the exact public JMdict and Tatoeba upstreams, can seek a license for the exact CJKI data family, and can contract with the disclosed OCR providers. It cannot yet claim language-stack parity for the unnamed components. Candidate replacements below are implementation options to evaluate, not evidence of equivalence.

## Evidence method and confidence

Evidence is classified as:

- **Verified-current:** first-party Nihongo/Apple material checked on 2026-08-02.
- **Verified-historical:** first-party Nihongo release notes that establish what a named release used or introduced.
- **Observed-historical:** an earlier local research record says an attribution was visible in-app on 2026-03-18, but the installed build and exact acknowledgment text have not been recaptured for this dossier.
- **Inference:** a conclusion consistent with evidence but not stated by Nihongo.
- **Unknown:** public and authorized evidence reviewed here does not identify the component.

No executable was extracted, decrypted, disassembled, or decompiled. No personal data or private device content was inspected.

## Corrections to preliminary research

The earlier source map was directionally useful but overstated several claims. This investigation makes these corrections:

- **JMdict and Tatoeba are verified Nihongo sources.** Jitendex is not; it is a third-party JMdict packaging candidate.
- **CJKI is verified for pitch accent.** The exact CJKI product and delivery are unknown. UniDic is a historical in-app attribution that needs current recapture.
- **Google Cloud Vision and OCR.space are verified online OCR providers.** They do not explain Nihongo's recognized-text grouping, parsing, or lower-accuracy offline mode.
- **KANJIDIC2, RADKFILE/KRADFILE, KanjiVG, and a particular Kanken mapping are not verified Nihongo sources by the reviewed public evidence.** Keep them as candidates.
- **Tatoeba is verified for example text, but it is not verified as the source of Nihongo's example-sentence audio.**
- **WaniKani/Tofugu, Kanji Alive, and Wiktionary are historical in-app observations, not current installed-build evidence.**
- **EDRDG's current license is CC BY-SA 4.0 and includes a regular-update obligation.** The preliminary shorthand “attribution-required” was incomplete.

## Blocking follow-up evidence

The following evidence is still required before the language stack can support a complete parity claim:

1. Capture the installed app version and its current Sources/Acknowledgments, pitch-accent attribution, audio attribution, dictionary attribution, kanji-history citations, and licenses. Record screenshots/semantic evidence, not copied assets.
2. Pin JMdict and Tatoeba snapshots and compare stable IDs plus a synthetic sample against the installed build to identify local additions, deletions, transformations, and example selection.
3. Run a fixture matrix for exact/partial English, kanji, kana, romaji, ambiguous romaji, misspelling, punctuation, whitespace, multi-word text, inflected verbs/adjectives, irregular forms, names, numbers, symbols, and no-result queries. Capture order, grouping, labels, and offline behavior.
4. Characterize token boundaries and lemma selection using synthetic sentences that isolate auxiliaries, particles, compounds, alternative writings, conjugations, punctuation, and ambiguity.
5. Capture every conjugation-table row for representative verb/adjective classes and irregulars, then test whether those same forms reverse-search to the same lemma.
6. Ask CJKI to identify the licensable dataset corresponding to Nihongo's displayed fields and quote mobile distribution, offline bundling, update, and derivative-display rights. Do not assume the public Japanese Phonetic Database is the exact product.
7. Crosswalk a sample of installed human-audio clips to the Tofugu and Kanji Alive public sets using stable metadata or authorized hashes; capture voice selection, cache, download, fallback, and network behavior.
8. Test online and offline OCR separately with purpose-created images. Preserve provider choice, connectivity, recognized strings, provider geometry where observable, Nihongo's tappable groups, lemma targets, errors, retries, and privacy disclosure.
9. Probe handwriting with controlled stroke order, order reversal, partial drawings, kana/non-jōyō characters, extra strokes, and erase/cancel states. The result should define behavior without guessing the recognizer.
10. Crosswalk kanji fields and stroke paths against KANJIDIC2, RADKFILE/KRADFILE, and KanjiVG samples. A match can raise confidence but must not be generalized to the whole source without a current attribution or audited denominator.

## Dossier decision

Use the following implementation-handoff language unless later evidence supersedes it:

- **Verified exact upstreams:** JMdict dictionary data and Tatoeba example-sentence data, subject to pinned versions, provenance, current license obligations, and proof of Nihongo-specific transformations.
- **Verified named licensed provider:** CJKI for pitch accent, but the exact dataset and reuse rights require direct confirmation and contract.
- **Verified online service providers:** Google Cloud Vision and OCR.space for OCR, with explicit image-upload privacy/network behavior.
- **Historically attributed but not current-pinned:** UniDic, WaniKani/Tofugu audio, Kanji Alive audio, English/Japanese Wiktionary, and Shinjigen.
- **Unknown/proprietary behavior:** search ranking, commonality weights, tokenizer, parser, lemma resolution, deinflection, conjugation rules, handwriting recognition, offline OCR, recognized-text grouping, kanji decomposition/visual-history compilation, stroke data, TTS configuration, and audio-selection/fallback logic.
- **Candidate substitutes:** KANJIDIC2/RADKFILE/KRADFILE, KanjiVG, UniDic with a measured analyzer, Apple Vision, and Apple speech synthesis. Record each as a proposed substitute until fixture comparison and stakeholder approval establish an acceptable variance.

All ingested facts should remain **Language Reference Data** with source/version/license provenance independent of provider schemas. Tokenization, OCR, speech, and other replaceable algorithms should remain behind focused Shared Capability interfaces, consistent with `docs/adr/0001-language-capability-boundaries.md`.
